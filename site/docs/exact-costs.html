which for 2 and 50 is 0.038. Nobody guesses 0.038.

threshold= and unsure_band=(lo, hi) are there if you already know the number you want. Mixing exact costs with posture words in one call is an error — one of them would have to win silently.

The trap in that formula

The expected cost of asking a person is flat in p, while the cheaper of yes and no peaks where those two lines cross. Put cost_human above that peak and there is no probability at all where a person is worth asking: the third branch you carefully wrote is unreachable, silently. The ceiling is

1.92 for 2 and 50. A cost_human of 5 never fires; 1 does. gut warns when you cross it, and Policy.max_useful_cost_human tells you where it is. This is not theoretical — an early demo of this library shipped with exactly that mistake, and sent zero tickets to a human with no error anywhere.

The postures cannot do this to you. They are defined as bands and the costs derived, and a band is reachable whenever its ends are in the right order.

A cost model is a claim about these probabilities. Change the backend and the same costs can land somewhere else on the new model's distribution; see limitations.
